Three things we refuse to speed up

The bloom

Thirty seconds of nothing happening, on purpose. Our head barista once extended it to ninety because the light was nice.

The roast

We roast in one-kilo batches on the riverbank and stop at first crack plus a considered pause. Beans rest four days before they meet a grinder.

The handover

Your cup is placed on the counter, not slid. Eye contact is made. This adds roughly eleven seconds and is the entire point.

Chico

Head Barista

Has never pulled a rushed shot. Once let a queue of eleven people wait while he finished a thought about water temperature. They all came back.

Mochi

Sleeps beside the drum and wakes at first crack, every time, without a timer. We have stopped asking how.

Dumpling

Tuna

How a coffee bar ends up run by capybaras

In 2019 a secondhand roaster fell off a truck near the river. By the time anyone came back for it, the capybaras had read the manual, worked out first crack by ear, and sold nine cups to a confused cyclist.

We tried to make it a normal café. We installed an ordering app. We put up a sign about efficiency. Both were quietly eaten within a week.

So we stopped. There is no app, no queue-management strategy, and no second location. There is one bar, on one bank, and it opens when Chico gets up.

“The coffee is genuinely excellent and it took twenty minutes and I have never felt calmer about anything.”

— a review we have laminated and hung above the grinder
